# Truss chassis prototype for a 349cc liquid-cooled single

Minimal triangulated space frame, every joint a truss node. Engine is **not designed**: it is a placeholder box with mount nodes you move later. Generated by `frame.py` (all numbers below come from it).

Axes: x forward, y left, z up, origin = swingarm pivot centre, millimetres.

For comparison, the Triumph Scrambler 900 is seat 790, wheelbase 1445, rake 25.6 deg, trail 109, 19/17 in wheels. This frame is a neutral naked-bike posture, not scrambler-style.

## 2. Truss rules used

- Straight tubes only. Member centrelines meet at one point at every node, so members carry axial load only.
- Rank of the rigidity matrix is 39 for 15 nodes (3j - 6 = 39): **isostatic, 39 members, none redundant**. Removing any member would make a mechanism.
- Seat (nodes C, S), tank (S, HT), footpegs (P), shock (M), swingarm pivot (D) and engine (E, F) all attach at nodes, never mid-span.
- Engine mount cross tubes: E-E passes over the engine, F-F passes behind the engine. No tube enters the engine box (min gap 26.8 mm, requirement 10 mm).
- The steering head tube (HT-HB, 50 x 3 mm) is the one non-truss element: it is a rigid link between two nodes.

## 5. Load cases and method

Pin-jointed 3D truss, stiffness method (numpy). Sprung mass 210 kg: rider 90, engine 60, tank/fuel 20, misc 30, plus tube mass. Supports only remove rigid-body motion; reactions come out as 0.0 N, which confirms each case is self-equilibrated.

| Case | Front wheel N | Rear wheel N | Shock axial N | Max node movement mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static 1 g | 1,040 | 1,018 | 2,321 | 0.19 |
| Bump 3 g (vertical) | 3,119 | 3,055 | 6,962 | 0.56 |
| Braking 1 g | 1,934 | 125 | 284 | 0.23 |

Front wheel load enters the head bearings (HT, HB) through the fork. Rear wheel load enters through the shock (node M) and the swingarm pivot (D), from the swingarm moment balance (shock lower mount x = -300).

Result: minimum yield safety factor **3.76**, minimum buckling safety factor **6.47**, no failing members.
Cornering is not a separate case: at 1 g lateral the bike leans about 45 deg and the frame sees roughly a 1.4 g vertical load, which the 3 g bump case covers for axial forces. Lateral kicks and torsion are not analysed.

## 6. Fabrication (cheapest route)

- Total tube: 19 x 1.6 mm 12,975 mm, 28 x 2 mm 320 mm, 50 x 3 mm head tube 140 mm. Tube mass 9.8 kg.
- `cutlist.csv` lists each member's centreline length, the cut length (axis pierce to pierce) and the included angle and mating tube at each end. Cope (fishmouth) each end onto the mating tube, trim to fit.
- Plates, 6 mm mild steel, flat patterns in `frame.dxf`: engine lug 70 x 90, hole 12 (x4); swingarm pivot plate 110 x 150, hole 20 (x2); footpeg plate 50 x 60, hole 10 (x2); shock plate 60 x 80, hole 12 (x1). A local laser or CNC shop is fine; JLCCNC can cut them too. JLCCNC is not a tube-frame service.
- Order: tack the two head nodes and the pivot tube in a flat jig first, then build each side, then the cross tubes (D-D, E-E) and the M node, then weld out. Check diagonals and the node positions against `frame.step` before final welding.
- Rough cost guess, not sourced: tube about 10 kg of steel, plates a few hundred rupees, welding and jig the main cost; plan roughly Rs 8,000 to 20,000 at a local workshop.

## 7. Limits and what changes with the real engine

- Idealised pin joints. Welded joints add some bending stiffness and some secondary bending; neither is modelled.
- Lateral loads, torsion, fatigue, weld quality, chain/drive loads and crash loads are not analysed.
- Engine mounts E, F and the envelope are placeholders. Edit `ENGINE`, nodes `E`, `F`, `P` in `frame.py` and rerun; the script re-checks rigidity, forces and clearance.
- **This is not a certified motorcycle frame.** A frame that carries a rider needs real FEA and physical testing by a qualified person before anyone rides it.
